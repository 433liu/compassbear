# Universal Research Grammar

Most strong scientific papers follow a transferable grammar:

1. **Need** — Why does the problem matter beyond one dataset?
2. **Barrier** — Why have existing approaches failed or stalled?
3. **Opening** — What conceptual or technical opportunity is now available?
4. **Move** — What did this work create, discover or connect?
5. **Mechanism / Rule** — Why does it work, and what can others reuse?
6. **Function** — What does it do in a relevant setting?
7. **Boundary** — What is validated, and what is not yet claimed?

## Diagnosing a weak manuscript

| Symptom | Likely missing grammar element |
|---|---|
| Reads like a data dump | Move or claim hierarchy missing |
| Introduction feels generic | Barrier or opening missing |
| Mechanism feels speculative | Mechanism-grade evidence missing |
| Application feels hyped | Boundary missing |
| Results feel chronological | Evidence ladder missing |
| Conclusion repeats abstract | Reusable rule or boundary missing |

## Recommended output

When asked to structure a paper, return:

1. Central claim
2. Need–Barrier–Opening–Move–Rule–Function–Boundary map
3. Proposed figure sequence
4. Main-text section headings
5. Risk notes
