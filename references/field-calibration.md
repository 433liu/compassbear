# Field Calibration

Before applying any writing or figure rule, calibrate to the field. The same evidence can be strong in one field and weak in another.

## Ask only when needed

Infer the field from the user's text whenever possible. Ask only if the standard of evidence, replication, mechanism or application maturity would change substantially.

## Calibration anchors, not universal thresholds

Use these examples as anchors. Do not treat them as rigid rules.

| Dimension | Calibration examples |
|---|---|
| Effect size | A large biological response, a modest clinical endpoint shift and a benchmark gain in ML should be judged against field-specific variance and baseline maturity rather than one universal threshold. |
| Replication | Chemistry often uses n=3 for measurements; biology often needs higher biological replicates; field/ecology studies require spatial and seasonal reasoning; ML needs appropriate splits and baseline comparisons. |
| Mechanism | Chemistry: targeted synthesis + spectroscopy + computation; biology: causal perturbation; ML: ablation + baseline + error analysis; engineering: prototype + stress test + characterization. |
| Application maturity | Proof-of-concept, controlled validation, field validation, deployment scenario and commercial readiness are different claim levels. |
| Self-citation | Emerging niches may require some self-citation, but self-citation must not replace international context. |

## Output format for calibration

When calibration matters, output:

| Claim | Field norm | Current evidence | Risk | Safer wording |
